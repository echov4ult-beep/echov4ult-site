What my AI crew actually shipped this month

The useful number is not how many agents I have. It is how many finished, checkable things made it out of the workshop.

By Chris Retherford · 9 minute read

I can make an AI team look busy forever.

Agents can draft plans, name files, produce status reports, and fill a dashboard with activity. None of that counts as shipped work. This month I used a simpler test: can another person open it, use it, read it, or play it?

Here is the receipt trail.

July 26: Full Party shipped

Full Party packages a 10-role virtual game studio into a kit for solo developers. The release included a free demo, paid editions, governance documents, workflows, commands, and templates.

The part I trust most is not the role count. Before selling it, we ran 22 acceptance scenarios against the installer and the installed behavior. The launch story and the failures are documented in the receipt trail and the acceptance-testing write-up.

July 27: Operator's Kit followed

Operator's Kit took the session habits I kept repeating and turned them into eight interlocking skills. It covers the beginning of a session, the quality floor during work, token discipline, and a clean ending.

This kit widened the audience beyond game development. The same discipline applies when I am building a website, investigating a bug, or trying to stop an AI session from becoming an expensive scavenger hunt.

July 28: Launch Crew became the third kit

Launch Crew installs a small marketing department: a CMO and five specialists, with a human approval boundary around publishing.

That boundary is deliberate. I want agents to research, organize, and draft. I do not want an unsupervised content machine speaking for me because a calendar cell says Tuesday.

August became a publishing test

The blog now has 21 published posts in the sitemap. The useful posts came from problems I was already dealing with:

  • What is graph engineering?
  • Laptop vs. VPS for always-on agents
  • What the Agent Plugins standard means for a working stack
  • Why autonomy defaults should be set on purpose

Publishing at this pace exposed stale navigation and an identity problem on the homepage. The writing was becoming broader than the storefront that contained it.

August 25: ViralLens moved onto the public site

ViralLens is a creator calibration experiment. It connects pre-publish creative judgment with post-publish TikTok performance so the next edit can learn from the last one.

The public page includes the connection and disclosure surfaces required for the TikTok workflow. The system behind it is still an active lab, so the homepage labels it that way. I would rather publish an honest state than turn every experiment into a launch announcement.

August 26: the website caught up with the work

The homepage at echov4ult.com now treats the brand as a project universe. The three kits remain available, but they sit beside ViralLens, V4ULT RUN, and the Build Log.

The public source repository is echov4ult-beep/echov4ult-site. It is a static GitHub Pages site with no framework and no build step. The current homepage is HTML, CSS, and a small shared JavaScript file.

The month also produced failures

I audited the wrong domain before redesigning the right one. A browser test used an ambiguous locator and stopped. An inline production smoke test exited without returning evidence. Each failure was logged, corrected, and rerun with a narrower check.

Those are small mistakes, but they explain the operating method better than a perfect retrospective would. The crew does not earn trust by avoiding every error. It earns trust by making errors visible and proving the correction.

What I am taking into the next month

The site needs to stay an edited public record. A project gets a card when it has a clear job, an honest state, and something another person can experience. Private experiments can remain private until they meet that bar.

I also want fewer parallel launches. One public focus with several operating projects is easier to understand than a wall of equally urgent status badges.

For now, start at the project universe, play V4ULT RUN, inspect the site repository, or read the free guides.
