Launch notes

We shipped Full Party five days early. Here's the receipt trail.

2026-07-26 · echov4ult

The kit existed for weeks, finished and unlaunched. What finally shipped it wasn't motivation — it was process.

Full Party — our AI game studio kit for Claude Code — went live on Gumroad on July 26, five days ahead of the July 31 target we'd set. This post is the honest version of how, because the how is the product's own pitch.

The embarrassing part first

The kit was done weeks before launch. Ten agents, thirty workflows, documentation, two editions, an installer — finished, tested, and sitting in a directory earning nothing. If you're a solo builder you know this state intimately: the gap between "built" and "shipped" isn't work, it's decisions nobody is forcing you to make.

What closed the gap was pointing our own governance loop at the launch itself, and treating "list the product" like any other feature: proposals, verdicts, a decision log, and hard gates.

The gates it went through

  • A rename with a paper trail. The working title didn't say what the product was. The rename to Full Party swept every file, both editions were rebuilt with the kit's own build scripts, and the acceptance suite was re-run afterward — 22/22 scenarios passing on the renamed build, not on a stale log from before the change.
  • A claims audit. Every number in the listing copy — 10 agents, 30 workflows, 22 commands, 27 templates — was re-verified against the shipped files, not the README's memory of them. One stale test log was caught claiming an old count; the suite was re-run live and the copy corrected.
  • A legal pass. License files, disclaimer language, and claims-vs-reality checks, with the legal entity name printed in every license before the listing went up.
  • A file-integrity check after listing. Post-launch, we verified each pricing tier delivers exactly its own zip — and caught a version-file mix-up before a single sale happened.

What we didn't do

No launch discount theater, no countdown, no invented testimonials — the product page has zero social proof because at launch we had zero customers, and we'd rather show you a Quality Critic failing our own crafting-system example than a wall of five-star quotes we wrote ourselves.

The lesson worth stealing

"Finished but unlaunched" is a governance failure, not a courage failure. The fix is the same one that works inside a game project: write the decision down, give a gate a date, and make "no" cost a recorded verdict instead of a vague feeling. That loop — roles, verdicts, decision log — is exactly what Full Party installs for game projects. We're just the first customer.
