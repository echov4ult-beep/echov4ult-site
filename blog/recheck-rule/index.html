The recheck rule: nothing ships checked once

2026-07-26 · echov4ult

One house law, adopted after enough AI work failed silently: every artifact gets a second, adversarial pass before it counts as done.

Here is the full text of the most valuable rule in our studio:

Everything built gets rechecked more than once — and the second check tries to prove the work wrong, not confirm it right.

That's it. No framework, no tooling. And in one launch week it caught three real problems that a single check had already missed.

Why AI work needs this specifically

Human mistakes tend to be loud — a crash, a typo, a missing file. AI mistakes are quiet and plausible: the confident summary of a file it misread, the test log from last Tuesday presented as today's, the claim that survives because it sounds like something that would be true. A first pass — by a human skimming or the same AI confirming itself — is tuned to accept plausible output. Only an adversarial pass is tuned to reject it.

What it caught, concretely

  • A stale test log. Product copy claimed the installer's acceptance suite passed — citing a log from an older, smaller suite. The recheck's question wasn't "does a log exist?" but "does a fresh run produce this result?" It did — 22/22 — but the claim was only true after re-running, and the log was refreshed.
  • A phantom feature. Marketing copy referenced a free bonus pack that appeared in early drafts and nowhere in the actual product. Confirmation reads that sentence and nods; adversarial reading asks "show me this in the shipped zip" and can't.
  • A pricing ghost. Draft posts mentioned a launch discount that no one had actually approved. The recheck rule's framing — every claim needs an owner — killed it in minutes.

How to run it without doubling your work

  • Change the instruction, not just the runner. "Review this" produces agreement. "Find what's wrong with this — assume something is" produces findings. Same model, opposite posture.
  • Recheck claims against sources, not summaries. The second pass re-opens the file, re-runs the command, re-reads the listing. Never let it check the first pass's notes.
  • Log it. Ours writes a RECHECK line with what was checked and what changed. The log is what makes the habit auditable — and skippable-proof.
  • Scale it to stakes. A one-line fix gets a glance. Anything customer-facing gets the full adversarial pass. Public claims get sources.

We liked the rule enough to build it into the product: Full Party's Quality Critic is the recheck rule with a job title — a role whose only function is to fail work that isn't ready, on a verdict scale, in writing. The worked example ships with a failure on purpose. That's not a bug in the demo; it's the entire point.
