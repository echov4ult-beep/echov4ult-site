What it is

A decision-making layer on top of the tools you already use

Not a game engine, not a framework, not a prompt pack. Full Party structures how your AI assistant works on your game — Godot, Unity, Unreal, custom, or no engine at all.

Roles

Ten specialists, one question each

Studio Director, Game Director, Art Director, Lead Engineer, Code Reviewer, QA Director, Quality Critic, Player Advocate, Narrative Designer, Marketing Strategist — each with a verdict scale and hard boundaries.

Governance

Who decides what, in writing

Five governance documents: an authority map, a decision protocol, handoff rules, escalation playbooks, and a Definition of Done. Decisions get recorded, not re-litigated every session.

Workflows

Concept to release-check

30 documented workflows and 22 slash commands — /game-vision, /feature-proposal, /qa-plan, /release-check — plus 27 pre-structured templates for every document the studio produces.

The honest part

Receipts, not vibes

Worked example

You see the governance loop fail, then work

The included fictional project, Lanternfall Grove, runs 11 scenarios from onboarding to weekly review — including the part most demos hide: the Quality Critic rejecting a crafting system (Major Revisions Required), the revision, and the re-review to approval.

Tested installer

22/22 acceptance scenarios passing

Clean installs, paths with spaces, pre-existing agent files, upgrades with backup, uninstall, reinstall. If a same-named file already exists in your project, the installer lists the conflict and stops without changing anything.
